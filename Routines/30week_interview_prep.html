Interview format — Google India L5 (Vertex AI Platform / Cloud AI)
ScreenRecruiter call + Online Assessment (45 min)+
FORMAT
Google India starts with a recruiter call (background + role fit), then an online coding screen via their own platform. 1–2 Medium problems, 45 minutes. Your aim is a clean correct solution with good complexity analysis — not just working code.
Your hook for the recruiter call: "I have 2+ years of production Vertex AI experience — I've built LLM pipelines, routing infrastructure, and agentic systems on top of Vertex in a professional context. I'd specifically like to talk to the Vertex AI Platform team."
R1–R33 Coding Rounds — LeetCode Medium/Hard (45 min each)+
GOOGLE'S SPECIFIC BAR
Google has the highest coding bar of all three. They expect optimal solutions, not just working code. Three separate rounds, each with a different interviewer. Problems lean toward: Graphs, Trees, Dynamic Programming, String manipulation, and occasionally Bit manipulation.
WHAT MATTERS AT GOOGLE
Talking through your approach before coding. Explaining time/space complexity clearly. Identifying edge cases proactively. Moving from brute force → optimal, narrating the transition. Google interviewers score on communication as much as correctness.
PRIORITY TOPICS
Graphs — BFS/DFS/DijkstraTrees — all traversals + LCADP — 2D grid, intervalsHash maps + sliding windowHeaps / Priority queue
Google will ask Hard problems at L5. You need 60+ LeetCode problems across Medium and Hard to be comfortable. NeetCode Blind 75 + NeetCode 150 is the minimum prep set. Quality over quantity — understand every solution deeply, don't just collect solved problems.
R4System Design — Googleyness + AI Systems (45 min)+
WHAT TO EXPECT
For Vertex AI team specifically: "Design a production LLM serving platform at Google scale", "Design a RAG system for Google Workspace documents", "Design a multi-tenant AI model routing layer." Google wants to see distributed systems depth — not just knowing the components, but discussing CAP theorem, consistency models, and failure modes fluently.
Your LLM routing layer (5 providers, 3-tier cost routing, async pipeline) is directly relevant. Translate it to Google scale: "I'd start from what I've built in production — here's the architecture — and here's what changes when you go from hundreds to millions of requests/day." Google loves candidates who have real production reference points and can reason from them.
R5Googleyness & Leadership (45 min)+
GOOGLE'S FRAMEWORK
Google probes for "Googleyness" — intellectual humility, comfort with ambiguity, collaborative instinct, and genuine intellectual curiosity. They don't use rigid LPs. Questions are open-ended: "Tell me about a time you had to work with incomplete information," "When did you disagree with a technical decision, and what did you do?"
Intellectual curiosity: "Building the multi-cloud LLM routing layer — here's what I read, what I experimented with, what I was wrong about initially."

Ambiguity: "Designing the async pipeline before there were established patterns for LLM job queues — here's how I reasoned through the architecture with limited precedent."

Collaboration: "The catalog traversal agent — working with data stewards who had domain expertise I didn't, here's how I integrated their knowledge into the prompt design."
Google verdict: Hardest interview of the three — 3 coding rounds including Hard problems. Needs the most prep time (12–14 weeks to be genuinely ready). But the payoff is the strongest: best perks, best India culture score, highest comp ceiling, and working on Vertex AI — the exact platform you've been using in production. Apply at week 12–14.
