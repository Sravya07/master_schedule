## Root Cause Analysis

**Why re-injury happened:** Proprioceptors never fully rehabilitated after injury #1 (May 24). Ankle felt healed, but sensors were still offline — re-injured at 10+ weeks (Aug 4) during the same inversion movement.

**Prevention for injury #3:** Aggressive proprioceptive retraining throughout Phase 2–5, then lifetime maintenance.

## Phase 2: Gentle Strength + Proprioception (Aug 10–21)

### Session Structure

| Session | Time (IST) | Duration | Frequency |
|---------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Morning mobility | 6:00–7:00 AM | 20–25 min | Daily |
| Midday balance | 12:00–1:00 PM | 10–15 min | Daily |
| Evening strength | 5:00–6:00 PM | 25–30 min | 5–6×/week |
| Bed mobility | 9:00–10:00 PM | 10 min | Daily |

**Rest day:** 1 day/week (suggested: Wednesday or Sunday)

### Exercises

#### 1. Ankle Circles (daily, 2× = morning + evening)
- **Reps:** 20 each direction × 2 sets
- **How:** Sit comfortably, leg elevated, rotate ankle slowly (3–4 sec per circle), full range
- **Feel:** Increasing mobility, reduced "different" feeling
- **Note:** No equipment needed

#### 2. Ankle Alphabet (daily, 1×)
- **Reps:** Full A–Z, 1 set (~4–5 min)
- **When:** Afternoon session
- **How:** Sit or lie with leg elevated, write each letter in air with toes, slow & full range
- **Why:** Multi-directional ankle movement, most important for proprioceptor deficit
- **Feel:** "Different" feeling reduces each day

#### 3. Point & Flex (2× daily)
- **Reps:** 15 × 2 sets
- **How:** Sit, leg straight · point toes away (2 sec) → pull toward you (2 sec), controlled
- **Feel:** Calf + shin working, increasing mobility
- **Note:** No equipment

#### 4. Ankle Eversion — Uninjured Foot Method (MOST CRITICAL)
- **Reps:** 12–15 × 3 sets
- **When:** Mon / Wed / Fri / Sat evening
- **How:** 
  - Sit, both legs extended
  - Place uninjured foot against **outer edge** of injured foot
  - Push injured foot **OUTWARD** against resistance
  - Hold 3–5 sec at end position
  - Return slowly (3 sec)
- **Resistance:** LIGHT — uninjured foot provides gentle resistance, equal & opposite
- **Feel:** Outer ankle/lower calf working, mild burn = correct, sharp pain = STOP
- **Why CRITICAL:** This is the movement that failed during re-injury. Must be strengthened.
- **Alternative if no uninjured foot:** Dupatta/towel loop around ball of foot, hold ends in hands, push foot outward against towel

#### 5. Ankle Dorsiflexion — Dupatta/Towel Method
- **Reps:** 12 × 3 sets
- **When:** Tue / Thu evening
- **How:** 
  - Sit, leg extended
  - Loop folded dupatta/towel around ball of foot
  - Pull toes **toward you** against towel resistance (held in both hands)
  - Hold 2 sec, release slowly
- **Feel:** Shin muscle (tibialis anterior) working
- **Why:** Shin strength = ankle stability platform, prevents ankle drop
- **Alternative:** Use belt or scarf if dupatta unavailable

#### 6. Calf Raises (standing)
- **Reps:** 15 × 2 sets
- **When:** Mon / Wed / Fri evening
- **How:** 
  - Stand at wall/chair, light hand support
  - Rise onto toes, hold 2 sec at top
  - Lower **slowly** (3 sec)
  - Both feet together
- **Feel:** Calf working significantly, ankle stays stable
- **Progression (Week 3):** Reduce hand support to fingertips only
- **Note:** Bodyweight only

#### 7. Seated Single-Leg Lift
- **Reps:** 8–10 × 2 sets
- **When:** Mon / Wed / Fri / Sat
- **How:** 
  - Sit upright in chair
  - Lift injured leg 3–4 inches off floor
  - Hold ankle **neutral** (not pointed or flexed)
  - Hold 10–15 sec
  - Lower slowly
- **Feel:** Core + ankle stabilizers working, ankle "wakes up"
- **Why:** Trains proprioceptors while ankle is safely non-weight-bearing

#### 8. Standing Single-Leg Balance (DAILY PRIORITY)
- **Reps:** 8–10 reps × 2 sets (20–30 sec each)
- **When:** **DAILY** — midday + evening (7 days/week)
- **How:** 
  - Stand on injured leg
  - Uninjured knee bent
  - Light hand touch on wall (safety only — not weight-bearing)
  - Hold 20–30 seconds
  - Concentrate on ankle stability
- **Feel:** Ankle working hard, "different" feeling should improve with daily practice
- **Why CRITICAL:** Directly retrains proprioceptive system — **THE most important exercise for preventing re-injury #3**
- **Progression (Week 3):** Try with eyes **closed** for 10–15 seconds (much harder — use wall firmly)

### Phase 2 Do's and Don'ts

✅ **DO:**
- Wear brace during **ALL exercises**
- Walk on flat surfaces 10–15 min (brace on)
- Track balance hold time daily
- Progress to eyes-closed balance (Week 3)
- Ice after exercise if ankle feels warm
- Reduce brace indoors by Week 3

❌ **DON'T:**
- Remove brace during exercise
- Walk on stairs or uneven surfaces
- Skip daily balance training
- Rush into eyes-closed before Week 3
- Force range of motion — work pain-free only
- Remove brace outdoors until Phase 3

### Phase 2 Exit Criteria (readiness for Phase 3)

- ✅ Balance hold: 30+ seconds
- ✅ "Different" feeling: 7–8/10 (improving)
- ✅ Zero swelling
- ✅ Walk 15 min comfortable

## Phase 3: Progressive Strengthening (Aug 21–Sep 11)

**Start only when Phase 2 success criteria are ALL met.**

### New in Phase 3

- Increase eversion resistance (thicker band OR 15–18 reps)
- Progress to single-leg calf raises (injured leg)
- Add balance on unstable surface (pillow/yoga mat)
- Add standing balance with arm movements (dynamic)
- Start walking 15–20 min flat, with brace
- By Week 5: Reduce brace during indoor activities

### New Exercises

#### Single-Leg Calf Raises (Week 5+ only)
- **Reps:** 8–10 × 2 sets (injured leg only)
- **When:** Mon / Wed / Fri — Week 5 onward only
- **Condition:** Only if Week 4 two-leg calf raises (15 reps) feel easy
- **How:** 
  - Stand at wall, light support
  - Rise on toes of **injured leg only**
  - Hold 3 sec, lower slowly (3–4 sec)
  - Control is everything
- **Feel:** Significant calf burn on injured side = correct
- **Note:** Bodyweight only

#### Single-Leg Balance on Pillow (Proprioception Level 2)
- **Reps:** 5–6 × 2 sets (20–30 sec each)
- **When:** Daily, Week 4 onward
- **How:** 
  - Stand on injured leg on folded pillow or yoga mat
  - Light hand touch on wall (safety only — NOT support)
  - Balance 20–30 seconds
  - Do NOT use hand for support
- **Feel:** MUCH harder than flat ground, ankle working intensely = correct
- **Why:** Unstable surface forces deeper proprioceptive training, mimics real-world conditions
- **Alternative:** Folded bedsheet, towel, or any soft mat

#### Balance with Arm Movements (Dynamic Proprioception)
- **Reps:** 5 × 2 sets (10 sec each)
- **When:** 3×/week, Week 4 onward
- **How:** 
  - Stand on injured leg
  - Balance 10 seconds
  - Slowly raise **both arms** overhead (5 sec up)
  - Lower slowly (5 sec down)
  - Keep ankle stable throughout
- **Feel:** Ankle must stabilize while upper body moves — sport-realistic training
- **Why:** Trains ankle to stay stable during dynamic full-body movement

#### Step-Ups (Week 5+ only)
- **Reps:** 10 × 2 sets
- **When:** 2–3×/week, Week 5 onward
- **How:** 
  - Use bottom stair step (4–6 inches)
  - Step up with **injured foot FIRST**
  - Step down slowly
  - Hold railing for safety — NOT support
- **Feel:** Ankle, knee, hip all working together
- **Caution:** NO jumping, controlled stepping only, sharp pain = STOP immediately

### Phase 3 Do's and Don'ts

✅ **DO:**
- Increase resistance every 1–2 weeks (more reps or thicker band)
- Walk 15–20 min daily with brace
- Reduce brace indoors by Week 5
- Track balance improvement weekly
- **Buy resistance bands before Week 3 ends** (₹300–500, Amazon.in — essential for rest of protocol)

❌ **DON'T:**
- Jump from Phase 2 → 3 before gate criteria met
- Run or jog (not yet)
- Remove brace during exercise until Week 6
- Do single-leg calf raises before Week 5
- Force balance on pillow before comfortable on flat ground
- Skip the balance arm-movement exercise (it's critical)

## Red Flags: When to Stop and Regress

| Red Flag | Action | Likely Cause |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| Swelling returns (even 1–2/10) | Stop · elevate + ice · regress 1 phase | Too much too soon |
| Sharp ankle pain (not muscle burn) | Stop immediately · rest 48 hrs · reassess | Ligament stress |
| Ankle "giving out" during balance | Stop · return to Phase 2 balance only | Insufficient proprioception |
| "Different" feeling **worsens** | Reduce intensity · increase mobility work | Overload |
| Swelling doesn't resolve in 2–3 hrs | Seek medical evaluation | Possible Grade 2 progression |
| Pain increases week-on-week | Seek medical evaluation · consider physio | May need professional assessment |

### Seek Medical Help If:
- Swelling doesn't improve after 72 hours of strict Phase 1 protocol
- Sharp, worsening pain despite complete rest
- Ankle feels severely unstable even with brace
- **Third re-injury occurs** (must see orthopedic specialist)
- Pain persists beyond Week 8 with full compliance

**Recommended in Hyderabad:** Apollo Hospitals / Yashoda Hospitals — Orthopedic + Physiotherapy departments
